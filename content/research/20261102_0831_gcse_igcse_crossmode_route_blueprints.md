## 2. Qualification distinctions and exact assessment gates
- **AQA 8300**: Foundation grades 1–5 and Higher 4–9; three 90-minute/80-mark papers at same tier, P1 non-calculator and P2/P3 calculator [S9]. Higher S3 unequal-class histograms and P9 conditional probability are NOT Foundation-only objectives [S2,S3]. Foundation R5 part-to-part/part-to-whole ratios apply to mixtures [S1]. AQA AO1/AO2/AO3 weightings are Foundation 50/25/25, Higher 40/30/30 [S7].
- **Cambridge 0580 2028–2030**: official PDF is **version 2 published August 2026**, not search-indexed version 1. Core P1 noncalculator + P3 calculator, 90 minutes/80 marks each; Extended P2 noncalculator + P4 calculator, 120 minutes/100 marks each. Core C1.8 standard-form **calculations** P3 only (conversion is not the same restriction); C1.10 allows bounds on recorded values but excludes bounds of calculation results. Extended E9.7 frequency density and E2.13 composition/inverse/domain are Extended objectives [S4,S10]. Core and Extended formula lists appear on their respective papers.
- **Pearson linear International GCSE 4MA1**: two 120-minute/100-mark calculator-permitted papers at one tier; Higher 6.1A unequal-class histograms [S5]. Do not route 4MA1 questions as modular units.
- **Pearson modular International GCSE**: official **qualification cash-in codes** 4XMAF and 4XMAH, **unit codes** 4WM1F/01, 4WM2F/01, 4WM1H/01, 4WM2H/01. This is NOT a qualification code '4WM'. Each unit 120 minutes/100 raw marks, calculators permitted; Unit 1 Higher includes histograms, Unit 2 Higher includes calculus and cumulative frequency; Unit 2 Foundation includes ratio 1.7. Units must be same tier for cash-in. Pearson states there is **no step up in difficulty between Unit 1 and Unit 2**, although Unit 2 assumes relevant Unit 1 knowledge [S6].
- **UK GCSE formulae**: Ofqual decision **5 May 2026** retains formula sheets for the remaining lifetime of current GCSE mathematics specifications, including resits; this does NOT automatically set Cambridge/Pearson international arrangements, which must use their own specifications [S8].
- **Other qualifications**: Cambridge/Pearson A Level, Further Mathematics, IB AA/AI and TMUA were reviewed in rotations 14–18; this rotation adds no new unverified claims for them. Keep their prior component/cohort gates in latest-handoff and rotate back after this GCSE/IGCSE cycle.

### 1. AQA-F-R5 — AQA GCSE 8300 Foundation — part:part ratio and mixture
**Exact objective:** R5 divide quantities in part:part or part:whole ratios and apply ratio to mixing contexts [S1]. **Gate:** Foundation; any paper; P1 non-calculator, P2/P3 calculator. **Misconceptions:** Treat part:part as part:whole; add ratios without converting to quantities.
- **AQA-F-R5-D / Grove Daily:** 252 stickers are shared between two teams in the ratio 4:5. How many stickers does each team receive? **Independent editorial checkpoint:** 9 parts; 28 per part; 112 and 140.
- **AQA-F-R5-W / Grove Weekly:** A 360 mL drink contains concentrate and water in the ratio 2:7. A further 40 mL of water is added. Find the new concentrate:water ratio. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** Concentrate 80 mL, water 280+40=320 mL; 1:4.
- **AQA-F-R5-E / Grove Exam:** A 540 mL solution has syrup:water = 2:7. Exactly 90 mL of well-mixed solution is removed and then 30 mL of pure syrup added. Determine the final syrup:water ratio and explain why removing mixture alone would not change it. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** Initially 120:420. Removal takes 20 syrup and 70 water; after addition 130:350=13:35. Uniform removal preserves the original ratio.

### 2. AQA-H-S3 — AQA GCSE 8300 Higher — unequal-width histograms
**Exact objective:** S3 construct/interpret histograms with unequal class intervals; S4 infer approximate position within grouped data [S2]. **Gate:** Higher only; any paper; for P1 use calculator-friendly exact values. **Misconceptions:** Use frequency instead of density for height; mistake class width for endpoint.
- **AQA-H-S3-D / Grove Daily:** A histogram class spans 12 ≤ x < 17 and has frequency 18. Find its frequency density. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** Width 5, density 18/5=3.6.
- **AQA-H-S3-W / Grove Weekly:** Three histogram bars have (class, density): [0,10):1.4; [10,25):1.2; [25,35):2.1. Calculate total frequency and identify the modal class. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** Frequencies 14,18,21; total 53; modal class [25,35) (highest density).
- **AQA-H-S3-E / Grove Exam:** A histogram has bars [0,4): density 3, [4,10): density 5, [10,18): density 2. Estimate the upper quartile assuming observations are uniformly spread within each class; state why the result is only an estimate. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** Frequencies 12,30,16 total 58; upper quartile at 0.75×58=43.5, 1.5 into final class, width increment 1.5/2=0.75; estimate 10.75. Within-class values are unknown.

### 3. AQA-H-P9 — AQA GCSE 8300 Higher — conditional probability from tables
**Exact objective:** P9 calculate and interpret conditional probabilities using expected frequencies, two-way tables and Venn diagrams [S3]. **Gate:** Higher only; any paper; not a Foundation-only assessment. **Misconceptions:** Divide by whole population instead of conditioning set; double-count intersection.
- **AQA-H-P9-D / Grove Daily:** Of 60 students, 30 travel by bus and 18 both travel by bus and attend robotics club. Find P(robotics | bus). **Independent editorial checkpoint:** 18/30=3/5.
- **AQA-H-P9-W / Grove Weekly:** In a group of 90, 54 play an instrument, 48 play a sport and 30 do both. Find P(sport | instrument) and P(neither). **Independent editorial checkpoint:** 30/54=5/9; union 54+48−30=72, neither 18/90=1/5.
- **AQA-H-P9-E / Grove Exam:** Among 120 participants, 70 choose coding, 65 choose art and 35 choose both. Calculate P(coding | not art), P(exactly one choice) and the number who choose neither. Show the conditioning denominator. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** Coding-only 35, not-art 55, conditional 35/55=7/11; exactly one 35+30=65 → 13/24; neither 120−(70+65−35)=20.

### 4. C0580-C-C18 — Cambridge IGCSE 0580 Core — standard form
**Exact objective:** C1.8 use a×10^n, 1≤a<10; convert in/out; calculate with standard form only on Core Paper 3 [S4]. **Gate:** 2028–30 v2; conversion may appear P1/P3; numerical calculations in standard form P3 ONLY. **Misconceptions:** Incorrect exponent sign; allow coefficient ≥10; wrongly assign calculation to Core non-calculator P1.
- **C0580-C-C18-D / Grove Daily:** Write 0.000084 in standard form. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** 8.4×10^−5. P1/P3 eligible (conversion only).
- **C0580-C-C18-W / Grove Weekly:** A factory produces 3.6×10^5 fasteners per day for four days. Express the total in standard form. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** 4×3.6×10^5=1.44×10^6. Paper 3 only.
- **C0580-C-C18-E / Grove Exam:** A reservoir contains 7.2×10^6 litres of water. It is shared equally among 2.4×10^3 identical tanks. How many litres does each tank receive, in standard form? **Independent editorial checkpoint:** (7.2/2.4)×10^(6−3)=3×10^3 L. Paper 3 only.

### 5. C0580-C-C110 — Cambridge IGCSE 0580 Core — bounds on recorded measurements
**Exact objective:** C1.10 find lower/upper bounds of rounded data; explicitly NOT bounds of results of calculations [S4]. **Gate:** 2028–30 v2 Core P1/P3; do NOT ask area/product/quotient bounds at Core. **Misconceptions:** Include upper endpoint; mistake half-unit; confuse bounds of input with bounds of a calculation.
- **C0580-C-C110-D / Grove Daily:** A strip is recorded as 28 cm to the nearest centimetre. State its possible actual length as an interval. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** 27.5 ≤ L < 28.5 cm.
- **C0580-C-C110-W / Grove Weekly:** Two rods are recorded as 12.4 cm and 12.6 cm, each to the nearest 0.1 cm. Could the first actually be longer than the second? Justify with the separate measurement intervals. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** First [12.35,12.45); second [12.55,12.65); no.
- **C0580-C-C110-E / Grove Exam:** Parcel A is recorded as 1.20 kg to the nearest 0.01 kg. Parcel B is recorded as 1.2 kg to the nearest 0.1 kg. Can B be lighter than A? Can B be heavier? Explain using possible recorded-value intervals, without calculating bounds of a derived quantity. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** A [1.195,1.205), B [1.15,1.25); both comparisons possible.

### 6. C0580-E-E97 — Cambridge IGCSE 0580 Extended — frequency density
**Exact objective:** E9.7 draw/interpret histograms and calculate density = frequency ÷ class width [S4]. **Gate:** 2028–30 v2 Extended P2 no calculator / P4 calculator; no Core assignment. **Misconceptions:** Use frequency for bar height; treat unequal widths as equal; forget continuous intervals.
- **C0580-E-E97-D / Grove Daily:** A histogram class 5 ≤ x < 11 contains 21 values. Find the frequency density. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** 21/6=3.5.
- **C0580-E-E97-W / Grove Weekly:** Histogram bars [0,3), [3,8), [8,14) have densities 4, 2.4 and 1.5 respectively. Find all class frequencies, the total, and the modal class. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** Frequencies 12,12,9; total 33; modal class [0,3) (highest density, not tied frequency).
- **C0580-E-E97-E / Grove Exam:** Grouped observations have intervals [0,5), [5,15), [15,25) with frequencies 10,30,20. Find histogram bar heights and estimate how many observations lie between 10 and 20, stating the assumption. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** Densities 2,3,2. Uniform within classes: 5/10×30 + 5/10×20=15+10=25.

### 7. C0580-E-E213 — Cambridge IGCSE 0580 Extended — functions and domain
**Exact objective:** E2.13 understand functions/domain/range, find inverse and composite gf(x)=g(f(x)) [S4]. **Gate:** 2028–30 v2 Extended only; composition domain restrictions required. **Misconceptions:** Reverse composition order; treat f^−1 as reciprocal; retain inadmissible branch.
- **C0580-E-E213-D / Grove Daily:** For f(x)=4x−7, find f^−1(x). **Independent editorial checkpoint:** f^−1(x)=(x+7)/4.
- **C0580-E-E213-W / Grove Weekly:** Let f(x)=2x+5 and g(x)=x²−1. Evaluate gf(3) and fg(3), and explain why they differ. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** f(3)=11, gf(3)=g(11)=120; g(3)=8, fg(3)=f(8)=21; order matters.
- **C0580-E-E213-E / Grove Exam:** Let f(x)=3x−4 and g(u)=u²+2 with domain u≥0. Solve g(f(x))=27 and state the restriction on x that makes the composition valid. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** f(x)≥0 → x≥4/3; (3x−4)²=25; positive branch 3x−4=5 gives x=3; negative branch x=−1/3 invalid.

### 8. P4MA1-H-61 — Pearson Edexcel linear International GCSE 4MA1 Higher — histograms
**Exact objective:** Higher 6.1A construct and interpret histograms for continuous variables with unequal class intervals [S5]. **Gate:** 4MA1/1H or /2H; both 120min/100 marks and calculator permitted; NOT modular 4WM. **Misconceptions:** Confuse 4MA1 linear with 4WM modular; equate bar area and height.
- **P4MA1-H-61-D / Grove Daily:** A class has width 8 and frequency 28. What histogram height represents its frequency density? **Independent editorial checkpoint:** 28/8=3.5.
- **P4MA1-H-61-W / Grove Weekly:** For classes [0,4), [4,10), [10,20), frequencies are 12,15,25. Find histogram densities and identify the modal class. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** Densities 3,2.5,2.5; modal class [0,4).
- **P4MA1-H-61-E / Grove Exam:** A histogram uses [0,5): density 2.4, [5,13): density 3, [13,18): density 1.6. Find the total frequency and the fractions in the middle class and in the final class. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** Frequencies 12,24,8; total 44; middle 24/44=6/11, final 8/44=2/11.

### 9. P4WM-U1H-61 — Pearson Edexcel modular International GCSE Unit 1 Higher — histograms
**Exact objective:** Unit 1 Higher 6.1A: histograms, unequal-width classes; no differentiation or Unit 2 cumulative frequency [S6]. **Gate:** 4WM1H/01; cash-in 4XMAH; calculator allowed; 120min/100 marks. **Misconceptions:** Assign Unit 2 objectives to Unit 1; confuse histogram height with frequency.
- **P4WM-U1H-61-D / Grove Daily:** A grouped-data class is 7 units wide and contains 28 observations. Find its frequency density. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** 28/7=4.
- **P4WM-U1H-61-W / Grove Weekly:** Histogram intervals [0,6), [6,10), [10,20) have densities 2, 3.5, 1.4. Calculate total frequency and identify the modal class. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** Frequencies 12,14,14; total 40; modal class [6,10) despite tied class frequencies.
- **P4WM-U1H-61-E / Grove Exam:** A histogram has [0,4): density 5, [4,12): density 2.5, [12,20): density 1.25. Find the percentage of observations below 12 and estimate how many of 150 similarly distributed observations would be below 12. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** Frequencies 20,20,10; total 50; below 12:40/50=80%; 0.8×150=120.

### 10. P4WM-U2H-34 — Pearson Edexcel modular International GCSE Unit 2 Higher — differentiation
**Exact objective:** Unit 2 Higher 3.4A–E: rate of change, integer-power differentiation, stationary points, linear kinematics [S6]. **Gate:** 4WM2H/01; cash-in 4XMAH; not Unit 1 or Foundation; 120min/100 marks calculator. **Misconceptions:** Confuse s,v,a; forget derivative of constant zero; stationary point type and domain.
- **P4WM-U2H-34-D / Grove Daily:** Differentiate y=5x³−4x²+7 with respect to x. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** dy/dx=15x²−8x.
- **P4WM-U2H-34-W / Grove Weekly:** For y=x³−6x²+9x+2, find the stationary points and classify each as a local maximum or minimum. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** dy/dx=3(x−1)(x−3); (1,6) local max, (3,2) local min.
- **P4WM-U2H-34-E / Grove Exam:** A particle has displacement s=t³−9t²+24t metres, 0≤t≤6 seconds. Find times when it is at rest, the accelerations then, and total distance travelled over six seconds. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** v=3(t−2)(t−4), rests at 2s and 4s; a=6t−18, giving −6 and +6 m/s². s(0)=0,s(2)=20,s(4)=16,s(6)=36; distance 20+4+20=44m.

### 11. P4WM-U2F-17 — Pearson Edexcel modular International GCSE Unit 2 Foundation — ratio and proportion
**Exact objective:** Unit 2 Foundation 1.7A–E simplify/divide ratios, direct proportion, real-world ratio problems [S6]. **Gate:** 4WM2F/01; cash-in 4XMAF; calculator and Foundation formula sheet; Unit 1 foundation prerequisites. **Misconceptions:** Confuse part:part and part:whole; treat adding an ingredient as scaling both.
- **P4WM-U2F-17-D / Grove Daily:** 168 beads are red and blue in the ratio 3:4. How many are red and how many blue? **Independent editorial checkpoint:** 7 parts; 24 per part; 72 red, 96 blue.
- **P4WM-U2F-17-W / Grove Weekly:** A 250 mL recipe uses concentrate:water = 2:3. How much additional concentrate and water are required to make 400 mL of the same mixture? **Independent editorial checkpoint:** Initially 100/150; target 160/240; add 60 mL concentrate and 90 mL water.
- **P4WM-U2F-17-E / Grove Exam:** A 480 g grain mixture contains rice:beans = 5:3. Only beans are added until the ratio becomes 5:4. Find the mass of beans added and explain why the rice mass is unchanged. **Independent editorial checkpoint:** Initial rice 300g, beans 180g. New beans 300×4/5=240g; add 60g. Rice not added or removed.
