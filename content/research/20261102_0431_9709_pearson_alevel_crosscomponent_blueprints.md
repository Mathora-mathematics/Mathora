## Original blueprint bank — 10 routes × 3 tiers = 30 original designs
All answers are editorial verification checkpoints, not confidential answer keys. Suggested marks are *proposed item marks*, not official mark allocations. ### A — Cambridge 9709 P1, AS/A: composition, circles and sector optimisation
- **A-D / Grove Daily / §1.2 / 3 marks:** Given f(x)=√(x−1) and g(x)=2x+3, write f(g(x)) and g(f(x)), stating each real domain. **Checkpoint:** √(2x+2), x≥−1; 2√(x−1)+3, x≥1. **Misconception:** treating the two domains as identical.
- **A-W / Grove Weekly / §1.3 / 6 marks:** The line y=3 cuts the circle x²+y²=25 at A and B. Determine both points, chord length and area of triangle AOB, O the origin. **Checkpoint:** (−4,3),(4,3); chord 8; area 12. **Misconception:** using radius 5 as the triangle height rather than 3.
- **A-E / Grove Exam practice / §§1.4,1.7 / 9 marks:** A sector has radius r cm and central angle θ radians, with perimeter 2r+rθ=30 cm. Express area in r, maximise it, and check the resulting θ is physically admissible (0<θ≤2π). **Checkpoint:** A=15r−r²; r=15/2; θ=2; maximum A=225/4 cm². **Misconception:** differentiating θ as if constant or omitting the angle constraint.

### B — Cambridge 9709 P2, AS Pure-only: numerical integration and iteration
- **B-D / Daily / §2.5 / 3 marks:** Evaluate ∫ from 0 to π/12 of sec²(3x) dx. **Checkpoint:** [tan(3x)/3] endpoints =1/3. **Misconception:** omitting the 1/3 chain factor.
- **B-W / Weekly / §2.5 / 7 marks:** Approximate ∫ from 0 to 1 of e^x dx using two equal-width trapezia. Compare with exact integral and explain the direction of error from curvature. **Checkpoint:** (1+2e^(1/2)+e)/4≈1.753931; exact e−1≈1.718282; overestimate ≈0.035649. **Misconception:** three ordinates imply three strips.
- **B-E / Exam practice / §2.6 / 9 marks:** For f(x)=x³−x−1, show a root lies between 1 and 2; apply x_(n+1)=(x_n+1)^(1/3), x_0=1, for three steps; explain why the displayed iterations alone do not establish convergence. **Checkpoint:** x1≈1.259921, x2≈1.312294, x3≈1.322354; root ≈1.324718; f increasing for x≥1. **Misconception:** reporting x3 as an exact root or asserting convergence without qualification.

### C — Cambridge 9709 P3, A Level: vectors and complex-plane loci
- **C-D / Daily / §3.7 / 3 marks:** Test whether u=(1,2,−1) and v=(3,−1,1) are perpendicular, with working. **Checkpoint:** u·v=3−2−1=0. **Misconception:** checking componentwise equality instead of scalar product.
- **C-W / Weekly / §3.7 / 7 marks:** L1 has position (t,0,1+t), L2 has position (1,s,3−s). Determine whether they are parallel, intersecting or skew; find the acute angle between their directions. **Checkpoint:** solving x,y gives t=1,s=0 but z values 2 and 3 disagree, hence skew; direction dot product −1, lengths √2 and √2, acute angle 60°. **Misconception:** assuming matching x,y coordinates imply intersection.
- **C-E / Exam practice / §3.9 / 9 marks:** In an Argand diagram find the locus satisfying |z−(2+i)|=|z−(−2+i)|. Intersect it with |z−(1+i)|≤3; give endpoint complex numbers and the maximum |z| on this intersection. **Checkpoint:** Re z=0; Im z between 1−2√2 and 1+2√2; endpoints i(1±2√2); max |z|=1+2√2. **Misconception:** treating modulus as an algebraic sign or failing to interpret the circle as a region.

### D — Cambridge 9709 P4 Mechanics: friction, connected bodies, energy
- **D-D / Daily / §4.1 / 3 marks:** A 4 kg block is in static equilibrium on a rough horizontal floor, coefficient μ=0.4, pulled horizontally by 9 N. With g=10, find actual friction and maximum possible friction. **Checkpoint:** R=40 N; actual friction 9 N, limiting 16 N. **Misconception:** assigning actual F=16 N.
- **D-W / Weekly / §4.4 / 7 marks:** Masses 3 kg and 2 kg are connected by a light string over a smooth pulley. From rest, determine acceleration, tension, speed and distance after 4 seconds (g=10). **Checkpoint:** a=2 m/s², T=24 N, v=8 m/s, s=16 m. **Misconception:** different string tensions or using total mass difference in place of net driving force.
- **D-E / Exam practice / §4.5 / 9 marks:** A 2 kg particle starts from rest on a horizontal surface. A 20 N horizontal force acts for 8 m against constant 5 N resistance, then the driving force is removed and the same resistance continues until rest. Find speed at force removal, further stopping distance, and total distance; state modelling assumptions. **Checkpoint:** net work 120 J, v=2√30 m/s; stopping distance 24 m; total 32 m. **Misconception:** subtracting resistance only after the driving force ends.

### E — Cambridge 9709 P5 Statistics 1: discrete distributions and approximation
- **E-D / Daily / §5.4 / 3 marks:** For independent trials with success probability 0.3, X is the number of the first successful trial. Find P(X=4). **Checkpoint:** (0.7)^3(0.3)=0.1029. **Misconception:** using four failures before success.
- **E-W / Weekly / §5.5 / 7 marks:** X~Bin(100,0.4). Approximate P(X≥45) by a Normal distribution; state the continuity correction and modelling justification. **Checkpoint:** μ=40, σ²=24, z=(44.5−40)/√24≈0.91856, upper tail≈0.17916. **Misconception:** using 45 instead of 44.5 or variance as SD.
- **E-E / Exam practice / §§5.3–5.4 / 10 marks:** A box has 3 blue and 2 red tokens. Two are drawn without replacement. X is number of blue tokens drawn. Tabulate its distribution, find E(X), Var(X), and P(X≥1), explaining the denominator. **Checkpoint:** P(0,1,2)=(1,6,3)/10; E=6/5; Var=9/25; P(X≥1)=9/10. **Misconception:** assuming independent replacement or E(X²)=E(X)².

### F — Cambridge 9709 P6 Statistics 2: Poisson and continuous density
- **F-D / Daily / §6.1 / 3 marks:** X~Poisson(2). Find P(X=0) and explain why it is not zero. **Checkpoint:** e^(−2)≈0.135335. **Misconception:** zero events means zero probability.
- **F-W / Weekly / §§6.1–6.2 / 7 marks:** Independent X~Poisson(3) and Y~Poisson(5). Find P(X+Y≤1), explaining which assumption permits the sum rule. **Checkpoint:** X+Y~Poisson(8), probability 9e^(−8)≈0.00301916. **Misconception:** adding Poisson means without independence.
- **F-E / Exam practice / §6.3 / 10 marks:** A continuous X has f(x)=kx(2−x) for 0<x<2, zero elsewhere. Find k, P(X<1/2), E(X), Var(X), and the median by symmetry. **Checkpoint:** k=3/4; P=5/32; E=1; Var=1/5; median=1. **Misconception:** treating f(1/2) as P(X=1/2), or demanding a named CDF for this Cambridge component.

### G — Pearson UK 9MA0 synoptic Pure: numerical methods and separable DEs
- **G-D / Daily / Pure §7 / 3 marks:** For x=t²+2, y=t³−t, find dy/dx at t=2. **Checkpoint:** (3t²−1)/(2t)=11/4. **Misconception:** dividing by t instead of dx/dt.
- **G-W / Weekly / Pure §9.3 / 7 marks:** Use Newton–Raphson twice for f(x)=x³−2x−2 from x0=2; show the recurrence and one possible failure condition. **Checkpoint:** x1=1.8, x2≈1.769948; f'(x)=3x²−2, derivative zero can cause failure. **Misconception:** substituting f rather than f' in denominator.
- **G-E / Exam practice / Pure §§8.7–8.8 / 9 marks:** A model obeys dy/dx=2xy, y(0)=3, x≥0. Find y(x), the first x where y=6, the rate dy/dx at that point and a limitation of extrapolating this model. **Checkpoint:** y=3e^(x²); x=√(ln 2); rate=12√(ln 2); unbounded growth may be unrealistic. **Misconception:** dropping constant after separation or taking a negative x outside stated domain.

### H — Pearson UK 9MA0 Paper 3 Statistics: inference and interpretation
- **H-D / Daily / Stats §3 / 3 marks:** Of 120 observations, 70 satisfy A, 50 satisfy B and 30 satisfy both. Find P(A|B). **Checkpoint:** 30/50=3/5. **Misconception:** denominator 120 rather than the B subgroup.
- **H-W / Weekly / Stats §5.3 / 7 marks:** Under H0, observations are Normal with mean 50 and known SD 8. A random sample of 16 has mean 54. Test H0: μ=50 against H1: μ>50 at 5% and interpret. **Checkpoint:** SE=8/√16=2, z=2, one-tailed p≈0.02275; reject H0 at 5%, evidence mean increased. **Misconception:** using individual-observation SD rather than SE; interpreting p as probability H0 is true.
- **H-E / Exam practice / Stats §§2,5 / 9 marks:** A study of 8 pairs reports r=0.81 and supplies a one-sided 5% critical r of 0.621. An existing regression line is y=2.3x+7, fitted using x in [2,8]. Assess evidence of positive association, predict at x=10, explain the extrapolation and why causal language is unjustified. **Checkpoint:** 0.81>0.621, significant positive correlation; predicted y=30; x=10 outside observed range; no causal proof. **Misconception:** deriving regression coefficients from r alone or claiming significance implies causation.

### I — Pearson IAL WMA12/01 P2: factor theorem, trigonometry, exponential models
- **I-D / Daily / P2.3 §2 / 3 marks:** Find the remainder on dividing 2x³−5x+7 by x−2. **Checkpoint:** f(2)=13. **Misconception:** substituting −2.
- **I-W / Weekly / P2.3 §6.2 trigonometry / 7 marks:** Solve 2sin²x−3sin x+1=0 for 0≤x<2π, explaining why there are three distinct solutions. **Checkpoint:** sin x=1/2 or 1; x=π/6,π/2,5π/6. **Misconception:** omitting the second-quadrant solution or counting π/2 twice.
- **I-E / Exam practice / P2.3 §§5.1–5.3 exponentials/logarithms / 9 marks:** Cooling is modelled by T(t)=20+60e^(−kt), t≥0. Given T(5)=50, find k, T(10), first time T=25, and explain why the model never reaches 20 at finite t. **Checkpoint:** k=(ln 2)/5; T(10)=35; t=5ln12/ln2≈17.9248; exponential remains positive. **Misconception:** taking ln of a negative difference or treating ambient limit as a finite-time crossing.

### J — Pearson IAL WMA14/01 P4: rational binomial, repeated poles and parametric area
- **J-D / Daily / P4.3 §4.1 / 3 marks:** Expand (1+2x)^(−1/2) through x² and state the validity interval. **Checkpoint:** 1−x+(3/2)x²+…; |x|<1/2. **Misconception:** integer-binomial coefficients or omitting convergence.
- **J-W / Weekly / P4.3 §2.1 / 7 marks:** Express (2x+3)/[(x+1)(x+2)²] in partial fractions with repeated linear poles; state exclusions. **Checkpoint:** 1/(x+1)−1/(x+2)+1/(x+2)², x≠−1,−2. **Misconception:** omitting the (x+2)^(−2) term.
- **J-E / Exam practice / P4.3 §§3.1,6.5 / 10 marks:** For x=t², y=t³+2 and 1≤t≤2, find the exact area under the curve between the endpoint x-values; find the tangent gradient at t=1 and explain the integration measure. **Checkpoint:** ∫_1^2 (t³+2)(2t)dt=92/5; dy/dx=3t/2 so gradient 3/2 at t=1; integrate y(dx/dt)dt, not y dt. **Misconception:** failing to transform dx.
