## Critical board/component differences and objective gates

| Route | Exact official objectives | Enforce before assigning | Likely misconceptions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cambridge 9709 P1 | §§1.1 quadratics/discriminant; 1.2 domain, range, inverse/composition; 1.4 circular measure; 1.6 **positive-integer** binomial, AP/GP and convergence; 1.7 differentiation incl. connected rates; 1.8 integration | 110 min/75 marks, calculator + MF19; valid for AS and A Level | Composition order; domain of inverse; signed vs geometrical area; radians vs degrees |
| Cambridge 9709 P2 | §§2.1 algebra, factor/remainder; 2.2 logs/exponentials and straight-line transformations; 2.3 trigonometry; 2.4 differentiation; 2.5 integration; 2.6 iteration | 75 min/50 marks; **P1+P2 AS-only and not carry-forward to A Level** | Taking logs of negative quantities; treating one iteration as proof of convergence; invalid P2→A route |
| Cambridge 9709 P3 | §§3.1 algebra and partial fractions; 3.4 implicit/parametric differentiation; 3.5 advanced integration; 3.7 3D lines/dot product; 3.8 separable differential equations; 3.9 complex numbers | 110 min/75 marks, **A Level P3** with P1 prerequisite; complex numbers belong here, not P1 | Squaring a complex number without i²=-1; omitting integration constant; assuming 3D lines intersect |
| Pearson UK 9MA0 Pure early strand | §§1 proof; 2 algebra/functions; 4 sequences/series; 7 differentiation; no board rule separating early content into Paper 1 | Both /01 and /02 are 120 min/100 marks and can draw from **all** Pure content | Treating counterexample as proof; missing coefficient cross-terms; dividing inequalities by variable of unknown sign |
| Pearson UK 9MA0 Pure advanced strand | §§7 implicit/parametric differentiation; 8 integration by parts/substitution; 9 numerical methods; 10 2D/3D vectors | Same **both-paper synoptic** gate; do not label Paper 2 as uniquely advanced | Omitting chain factor; treating numerical approximation as exact; missing signed area/units |
| Pearson IAL WMA11 P1 | P1.3 §§1 algebra, 2 lines, 3 trigonometry, 4 differentiation **no chain rule required**, 5 basic integration | 90 min/75 marks, calculator and formula booklet; prerequisite for P2 | Using chain rule beyond unit demand; confusing tangent and normal; adding a constant to definite integrals |
| Pearson IAL WMA12 P2 | P2.3 §§1 proof (exhaustion/counterexample), 2 factor/remainder, 3 circles, 4 sequences/series, 5 logs, 7 stationary points, 8 definite integrals/trapezium rule | 90 min/75 marks; P1 prerequisite | False universal claim from a few examples; GP index errors; confusing definite integral with total geometric area |
| Pearson IAL WMA13 P3 | P3.3 §§1 domain/inverse/composition and modulus, 2 trig, 3 logs/exponentials, 4 calculus, 5 integration, 6 iteration | 90 min/75 marks; P1+P2 prerequisites | Confusing f∘g order; ignoring exponential positivity; treating x_{n+1} as independent x |
| Pearson IAL WMA14 P4 | P4.3 §§1 contradiction, 2 partial fractions, 3 parametric geometry, 4 **rational-index binomial**, 5 implicit/parametric calculus, 6 integration/volume/differential equations, 7 3D vectors | 90 min/75 marks; P1–P3 prerequisites; separate from UK 9MA0 and Cambridge 9709 | Omitting |x| convergence restriction; assuming two nonparallel 3D lines meet; mixing up dx/dt and dy/dt |

**High-impact exclusions:** Cambridge 9709 P1 binomial is for positive integer powers, whereas Pearson IAL rational-index binomial belongs to **P4**. Cambridge 9709 P3 §3.1 explicitly includes partial fractions with an irreducible quadratic denominator; Pearson IAL P4 §2.1 expressly **does not require** quadratic factors such as x²+a. Pearson IAL P4 §6.5 allows parametric area but does **not** expect sketching a parametric curve from its equations. Pearson IAL P1 does not require chain rule, and area **between two curves** is explicitly described in P2 §8.2, so it is deliberately not placed in a P1-only blueprint. [S2,S4,S5]

## Original Grove Daily / Weekly / Exam blueprint bank
All prompts below are **new editorial constructions**, not copied from specimens. Daily ~2–5 minutes, Weekly ~6–10 minutes, Exam ~10–16 minutes (reasoning-rich **practice**, not a complete timed mock). Approximate editorial mark allocations must be ratified. Answers are for private examiner validation; do not expose them in a public student payload.

### Lane A — Cambridge 9709 P1, AS/A Level (2026–27 and 2028–30)
- **Daily A-D, §1.2 (3 marks):** For f(x)=2x+5 with x≥1, find f⁻¹(x) and its domain. **Check:** f⁻¹(x)=(x−5)/2, domain x≥7. **Trap:** assigning inverse domain x≥1 instead of original range.
- **Weekly A-W, §1.4 (6 marks):** A circular segment has radius 8 cm and subtends π/3 radians at its centre. Find its exact area by subtracting the triangle from the sector. **Check:** 32π/3−16√3 cm². **Trap:** using degree mode or taking sector area alone.
- **Exam A-E, §§1.1,1.3 (8 marks):** For which real m does y=mx+1 touch y=x²−4x+7 at exactly one point? Give both m values and corresponding contact x-coordinates. **Check:** (m+4)²−24=0, m=−4±2√6, x=±√6 with matching signs. **Trap:** only one discriminant branch; wrong discriminant sign.

### Lane B — Cambridge 9709 P2, AS Pure-only route
- **Daily B-D, §2.2 (3 marks):** Solve 5·3^(x−1)>20 exactly. **Check:** x>1+ln4/ln3. **Trap:** reversing inequality although ln3>0; losing the −1 shift.
- **Weekly B-W, §2.1 (7 marks):** f(x)=x³+ax²+bx+6. It is divisible by x−1 and leaves remainder 0 on division by x+2. Find a,b and factorise f fully. **Check:** a=−2, b=−5; f=(x−1)(x−3)(x+2). **Trap:** evaluating at +2 instead of −2.
- **Exam B-E, §2.2 (9 marks):** A positive-variable calibration model is y=kx^n. Measurements are (2,12) and (8,96). Determine k,n, predict y at x=18, state the gradient and intercept of ln y against ln x, and explain why the prediction is an extrapolation. **Check:** n=3/2, k=3√2, y(18)=324; gradient 3/2, intercept ln(3√2). **Trap:** using x/y as linear axes; extrapolation mistaken for observed data.

### Lane C — Cambridge 9709 P3, A Level only
- **Daily C-D, §3.9 (3 marks):** Find both square roots of 3+4i in Cartesian form. **Check:** ±(2+i). **Trap:** listing only the principal square root.
- **Weekly C-W, §3.1 (7 marks):** Decompose 1/[(x+1)(x²+4)] into A/(x+1)+(Bx+C)/(x²+4). State real-domain restrictions. **Check:** A=1/5, B=−1/5, C=1/5; x≠−1. **Trap:** assuming real roots of x²+4.
- **Exam C-E, §3.8 (10 marks):** A population P(t) obeys dP/dt=(1/5)P(1−P/100), with P(0)=20 and t≥0. Derive P(t), find the time at which P first reaches 50, and explain why the model approaches but does not exceed 100 for finite t. **Check:** P=100/(1+4e^(−t/5)); t=5ln4. **Trap:** missing separation/partial fractions, wrong initial constant or interpreting asymptote as a finite-time crossing.

### Lane D — Pearson UK 9MA0 Pure, early-prerequisite strand (both /01 and /02)
- **Daily D-D, §1.1 (2 marks):** Disprove "n²+n+5 is prime for every nonnegative integer n". **Check:** n=5 gives 35=5·7. **Trap:** trying to prove a universal statement from several positive cases.
- **Weekly D-W, §§2,4 (7 marks):** Find the coefficients of x and x² in (1+2x)^5(1−x)^3, then calculate their ratio. **Check:** 7 and 13; ratio 13/7. **Trap:** ignoring cross-products of the two expansions.
- **Exam D-E, §§1,2 (10 marks):** A rectangle has side lengths x and 9/x, x>0, so its area is 9. Prove x+9/x≥6 without differentiation, find the least possible perimeter, and solve for the widths meeting a perimeter limit of 13. **Check:** (x−3)²/x≥0; min perimeter 12 at x=3; 2≤x≤9/2. **Trap:** multiplying an inequality by x without establishing x>0; forgetting equality case.

### Lane E — Pearson UK 9MA0 Pure, advanced-prerequisite strand (both /01 and /02)
- **Daily E-D, §7 (3 marks):** Find dy/dx at (1,2) on x²+xy+y²=7. **Check:** −4/5. **Trap:** treating y as a constant while differentiating xy.
- **Weekly E-W, §§8,9 (8 marks):** Estimate ∫₀²(x²+1)dx using the trapezium rule with four equal strips. Find the exact integral and signed error; justify whether it is an overestimate. **Check:** trapezium 19/4, exact 14/3, error +1/12; convex curve. **Trap:** using five strips for five ordinates.
- **Exam E-E, §8 (9 marks):** Evaluate I=∫₀¹ x e^(2x)dx by parts, then find real a such that ∫₀¹(x+a)e^(2x)dx=e². Explain why the solution is unique. **Check:** I=(e²+1)/4; a=(3e²−1)/[2(e²−1)], unique since ∫₀¹e^(2x)dx>0. **Trap:** dropping factor 1/2 in integration by parts.

### Lane F — Pearson IAL WMA11 P1
- **Daily F-D, P1.3 §4 (3 marks):** For y=(3x−2)√x, x>0, find dy/dx at x=4 by first expanding into powers of x. **Check:** 17/2. **Trap:** unnecessarily requiring a product/chain rule that P1 does not demand.
- **Weekly F-W, P1.3 §§1–2 (7 marks):** Find both intersections of y=2x+1 and y=x²−3x+5, then the distance between them. **Check:** (1,3), (4,9), distance 3√5. **Trap:** solving x correctly but substituting into the wrong curve.
- **Exam F-E, P1.3 §§4–5 (9 marks):** A curve has f'(x)=6x²−8x+3 and passes through (1,4). Find f(x), the tangent and normal at x=2, and the normal's y-intercept. **Check:** f(x)=2x³−4x²+3x+3; point (2,9); tangent y−9=11(x−2); normal y−9=−(x−2)/11; intercept 101/11. **Trap:** missing integration constant; normal gradient not negative reciprocal.

### Lane G — Pearson IAL WMA12 P2
- **Daily G-D, P2.3 §5 (3 marks):** Solve 2^(2x−1)=7 exactly. **Check:** x=(1+ln7/ln2)/2. **Trap:** losing the factor 2 in the exponent.
- **Weekly G-W, P2.3 §4 (7 marks):** A geometric sequence has u₃=12 and u₆=96. Determine a, r, and S₈. **Check:** r=2, a=3, S₈=765. **Trap:** mistaking u₃=ar³ rather than ar².
- **Exam G-E, P2.3 §§4–5 (9 marks):** Payments follow A_n=100(0.8)^n pounds, n=0,1,2,… . Find the first index n for which A_n<£10; calculate the sum of payments strictly before that index; explain why the strict inequality matters. **Check:** n=11 (the 12th payment); S of n=0…10 is 500(1−0.8^11)≈£457.05. **Trap:** reversing the inequality on division by ln0.8<0; off-by-one sum.

### Lane H — Pearson IAL WMA13 P3
- **Daily H-D, P3.3 §1.2 (3 marks):** Find inverse and inverse domain of f(x)=ln(x−2), x>2. **Check:** f⁻¹(x)=e^x+2; domain all real numbers. **Trap:** using x>2 as the inverse's domain.
- **Weekly H-W, P3.3 §§1,3 (7 marks):** Solve e^(2x)−5e^x+6<0 exactly, explaining substitution and sign. **Check:** ln2<x<ln3. **Trap:** treating e^x as possibly negative.
- **Exam H-E, P3.3 §§3–4 (9 marks):** A model y=Ae^(kx) passes through (0,5) and (2,45). Determine A,k, find the x-value when y=135, and compute dy/dx there. **Check:** A=5, k=ln3, x=3, derivative 135ln3. **Trap:** k=3 rather than ln3; differentiating e^(kx) without k.

### Lane I — Pearson IAL WMA14 P4
- **Daily I-D, P4.3 §4 (4 marks):** Give the first three nonzero terms of (1+2x)^(−1/2) and its convergence condition. **Check:** 1−x+(3/2)x²+…, |x|<1/2. **Trap:** binomial coefficients for positive integers; omitting domain.
- **Weekly I-W, P4.3 §7 (8 marks):** Lines L₁: r=(1,0,2)+s(1,2,−1) and L₂: r=(0,1,4)+t(2,1,−2). Prove they are skew, then find the acute angle between their directions. **Check:** x-coordinate implies 2t−s=1 but z-coordinate implies 2t−s=2, impossible; direction vectors not parallel; cosθ=√(2/3), θ≈35.26°. **Trap:** assuming nonparallel lines intersect in 3D.
- **Exam I-E, P4.3 §§5–6 (11 marks):** For x≥0, a curve satisfies dy/dx=[2x/(1+x²)]y and y(0)=5. Solve for y. The region under the curve between x=0 and x=1 rotates about the x-axis; find the exact volume. **Check:** y=5(1+x²), V=π∫₀¹25(1+x²)²dx=140π/3. **Trap:** losing constant after separation; revolving y not y².
