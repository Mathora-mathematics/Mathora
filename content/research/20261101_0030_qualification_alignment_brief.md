## 2. Verified objectives and important exam-board differences

| Route / objective code | Learning outcome for authoring (paraphrase) | Boundary and likely misconception |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cambridge 0580 Core C8.1** | Single-event probability, complement and the 0–1 scale; read two-set Venns/tables | Probability notation is **not required** in Core; do not demand conditional-probability notation. |
| **Cambridge 0580 Core C8.2** | Use experimental relative frequency as an estimate and calculate expected frequency | Relative frequency is not certainty; expected count need not equal actual count. |
| **Cambridge 0580 Core C8.3** | Combine events using sample spaces, two-set Venns or tree diagrams | **Only with replacement** for combined events in Core. A without-replacement tree is not a Core-only task. |
| **Cambridge 0580 Extended E8.3–E8.4** | With/without replacement; conditional probability from diagrams/tables | Conditional notation and formula are **not required** even at Extended; avoid compulsory P(A\|B) symbolism. |
| **Pearson 4MA1 Foundation 6.3 A–J** | Language/scale, sample spaces, Venns, complements, exclusive addition and expected frequency | Do not put Higher-only simple conditional probability into Foundation. |
| **Pearson 4MA1 Higher 3.2 A–D** | Function mapping, f(x), domain/range, composition and inverses | fg means do g first; exclude denominator zeros; inverse requires appropriate domain restrictions. |
| **Pearson 4MA1 Higher 6.3 A–D** | Trees, independent events and simple conditional probability (e.g. draws without replacement) | Denominator changes after first draw; independence must not be assumed. |
| **AQA 8300 P6–P8** | Enumerate outcomes and compute independent/dependent event probabilities; tree representations | P8 is **additional Foundation content**, not automatically Higher-only. |
| **AQA 8300 P9** | Conditional probability interpreted from frequency tables, trees and Venns | **Higher-only**; conditional sample space must be restricted to given event. |

**Assessment-format non-interchangeability:**
- Cambridge 0580 **Core P1 non-calculator 90 min/80 marks** and **P3 calculator 90 min/80 marks**; **Extended P2 non-calculator 120 min/100 marks** and **P4 calculator 120 min/100 marks**. Core C–G content and Extended A*–C teaching targets; avoid treating them as UK 9–1 GCSE grade labels. Syllabus says Core C–G and Extended A*–E examination eligibility.
- Pearson 4MA1 linear **two 120-minute, 100-mark papers**, calculators permitted on both; Foundation 1F/2F versus Higher 1H/2H. Pearson 4XMA1 modular is a **different assessment route**; same content but separate units and cash-in rules. Never add a Cambridge-style compulsory non-calculator paper to 4MA1.
- AQA 8300 GCSE **three 90-minute/80-mark papers**: P1 non-calculator, P2 and P3 calculator; Foundation 1–5, Higher 4–9. Foundation topic weightings approximately Number 25%, Algebra 20%, Ratio 25%, Geometry 15%, Probability+Statistics 15%; Higher Number 15%, Algebra 30%, Ratio 20%, Geometry 20%, Probability+Statistics 15%. These are **qualification-wide approximate weights**, not paper quotas.
- Pearson 1MA1 UK GCSE is **not** Pearson 4MA1 International GCSE; do not map Pearson 1MA1 staged drafts to 4MA1.
- Cambridge 9709 and Cambridge Further 9231 are component-based with specific applied routes; Pearson UK GCE 9MA0/9FM0 and Pearson IAL are separate qualification families. Pearson IAL has unitised 75-mark/90-minute papers, not the UK linear A-level paper structure.
- IB **AA SL/HL** prioritises mathematical structure and argument; **AI SL/HL** prioritises modelling, data and technology. The current courses' **final assessment is November 2028**; first-assessment-2029 curriculum must not be used for current cohorts. AI external assessments use technology.
- TMUA is **two non-calculator 75-minute papers of 20 MCQs each**: applications and mathematical reasoning; no negative marking. Paper 2 logic includes converses, contrapositives, necessary/sufficient, negation and counterexamples, without requiring symbolic truth tables.

### Route C0580-C — Cambridge IGCSE Core, probability (new approved-coverage gap)

**Grove Daily** [C8.2; ~2–4 min; non-calculator]: use a new experimental tally (e.g. 48 successes in 80 fair trials) to estimate the success probability and the expected successes in a new batch (e.g. 90 trials). Require a sentence explaining that an expected frequency is not guaranteed. Misconceptions: 48/90; using the 80 as future population; rounding before multiplying.

**Grove Weekly** [C8.1 + C8.3; 6–9 min; non-calculator]: original coloured-token game, two draws **with replacement**, 5 tokens of one colour and 3 of another. Build a complete two-stage tree, compute the probability of matching colours by adding two disjoint branches, and compare to the complementary outcome. Misconceptions: silently reducing denominator to 7; adding probabilities along a path; treating matching colours as one branch.

**Grove Exam practice** [C8.2 + C8.3 + C1.11; 9–12 min; calculator allowed]: design a carnival stall with two independent spins, an entry fee and a prize payable for two specified outcomes; derive the win probability, predicted wins over a specified number of plays and expected gross takings. Include a reasonableness check and clear assumptions; no advanced expected-value distribution beyond Core. **Do not label this a full Paper 3 mock**.

### Route P4MA1-F — Pearson International GCSE Foundation, probability (new board-specific gap)

**Grove Daily** [6.3 D,H; 2–4 min; calculator]: a fresh two-set Venn frequency table with overlap and outside counts; ask for a complement probability from the universal population, then check total. Misconception: treating the overlap as two separate pupils.

**Grove Weekly** [6.3 E,F,I,J; 6–9 min; calculator]: construct a sample space for two fair spinners with unequal category frequencies; find two mutually exclusive winning outcomes, then predict wins in a larger number of plays. Ensure each equally likely elementary outcome is listed and sample-space probabilities sum to one.

**Grove Exam practice** [6.3 A–J; 10–12 min; calculator]: a school survey of preferred after-school clubs with a two-way table; calculate a complement, an exclusive union and expected frequency for a larger cohort; discuss whether the extrapolation is reliable. Avoid without-replacement conditional questions in Foundation.

### Route P4MA1-H — Pearson International GCSE Higher, probability/functions (new board-specific gap)

**Grove Daily** [6.3 C; 3–5 min; calculator]: two draws **without replacement** from a bag containing 5 green and 4 yellow counters; condition on the first draw being yellow and ask for the second-draw green probability. Distractors should target denominator 9 versus 8 and confusion between conditional and joint probabilities.

**Grove Weekly** [3.2 B–D; 7–9 min; calculator]: define one affine function and one reciprocal function with a forbidden input. Ask for the composite in the correct order, its domain restriction and whether the inverse of the affine function is valid on its stated domain. Avoid reusing the existing Cambridge Extended composite-function draft wording.

**Grove Exam practice** [6.3 B–D; 10–13 min; calculator]: 4 black and 5 white counters, two draws without replacement; compare the probability of exactly one black with the corresponding **with-replacement** version, then estimate successes in 90 independent repeated experiments. Explicitly restore the bag between separate experiments; distinguish within-trial from between-trial dependence.

### Route A8300-H — AQA GCSE Higher, conditional probability (distinct from current surds/quadratics releases)

**Grove Daily** [P9; 3–5 min; calculator]: original 2×2 frequency table of two overlapping school activities; ask for the probability of activity B **given** participation in A. Use only the A column/row as denominator. Misconception: use whole sample size.

**Grove Weekly** [P6 + P8 + P9; 7–10 min; non-calculator]: compare an event probability read from a Venn diagram with the conditional version; explain why the two values differ and check the intersection count. Require a brief explanation, not just a fraction.

**Grove Exam practice** [P8 + P9; 10–14 min; calculator]: design a two-stage reliability test with different pass probabilities depending on an initial screening outcome. Ask for overall pass probability and probability of a specific screening group given success; insist on a complete frequency tree or probability tree and an explanation of the changed sample space.
