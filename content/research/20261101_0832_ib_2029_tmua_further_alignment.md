The IB official Mathematics page, last updated **17 September 2026**, explicitly says the **current** AA/AI courses are last assessed **November 2028**; the **new** courses begin teaching in **2027** and are first assessed **May 2029**. Treat \`exam_session_year\` and \`syllabus_version\` as required for every IB item and learner. ## 2. TMUA: current test-specific gates and accessible public examples
The [UAT-UK 2026/27 specification](https://uat-wp.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/03165619/TMUA_Content_Specification.pdf) is explicitly labelled for **October 2026 and January 2027**. **Paper 1**: Section 1 maths application, 20 MCQ, 75 min. **Paper 2**: Section 1 maths plus Section 2 reasoning, 20 MCQ, 75 min. No calculator, no formula booklet, equal item weighting, no negative marking. Paper 2 exact requirements: **Arg1** inclusive-or, converse and contrapositive; **Arg2** necessary/sufficient; **Arg3** universal/existential language; **Arg4** negation; **Prf1** direct, cases, contradiction, disproof by counterexample; **Prf2** deductions. No formal truth tables or symbolic logic notation expected. Section 1 includes **MM1.5** linear/quadratic inequalities, **MM1.6** factor/remainder theorem, **MM2.1** recurrences, **MM4.6** trigonometric equations and **MM7.1** signed-integral versus area.

Public [UAT-UK preparation materials](https://esat-tmua.ac.uk/prepare/) link to the **Pearson computer specimen/practice player**, free notes on logic/proof and archived papers. The current official content PDF **does not specify the number of answer options**. The browser-readable official preparation page does not reveal the specimen player's per-item choice count. **Do not assert that a 4-option bank item is an authentic TMUA mock.** Human/desktop specimen-player verification remains a blocker. TMUA scores are scaled (1–9), not simply percentages: do not display a fabricated official score from a private raw-mark practice paper.

**Misconceptions:** Converse does not follow from an implication; \`or\` is inclusive; “some” means at least one; negating \`all\` means there exists a counterexample; dividing by an expression that may be zero loses cases; a plausible example does not prove a universal statement.

## 4. Original editorial blueprint register — **36 designs, 12 routes × Daily/Weekly/Exam**
**Tier contract:** Daily = 2–5 min narrow skill with one explicit method cue; Weekly = 6–12 min with 2–4 connected steps and a check; Exam = 9–16 min original reasoning-rich **daily practice**, not an approved timed mock. Each design needs a separate private verified answer and marking rubric before release. Seeds are intentionally unlike the earlier IB quadratic-domain/optimisation, AI regression/residual/logistic/Poisson, TMUA cancellation/quadratic discriminant, Further matrices/roots/telescoping and A Level mechanics/CLT sets. Use unique scenario and symbolic fingerprints, not cosmetic number changes.

| **IB AA HL current 2021–28** (complex numbers; 2029 A4 HL) | Find modulus and principal argument of \`-1+i\`; justify quadrant. | Find all four roots of \`z⁴=-16\`, draw the Argand geometry, and verify their product. | A transmitter uses four equally spaced complex phasors; deduce their vector sum, rotate by a fixed argument, and show cancellation algebraically rather than relying on a sketch. | Principal vs all arguments; omitted roots; multiplying arguments rather than adding. |

| **TMUA P2** (Arg1–4, Prf1–2) | Write the contrapositive of “if an integer is divisible by 6, it is divisible by 3.” | Analyse a false universal claim “if \`ab\` is divisible by 6, one of \`a,b\` must be divisible by 6”; provide a minimal counterexample and distinguish converse. | Decide whether “\`12 | n²\` if and only if \`6 | n\`” holds for all integers; give a valid parity/prime-divisibility argument, then critique a flawed converse-based proof. | An example is not a proof; converse confusion; quantifier mistakes. |

| **Cambridge 9231 P3** (§3.3 circular motion) | Compute radial acceleration for speed 9 m/s on radius 3 m and state inward direction. | A conical pendulum with given length and angle: resolve vertical/horizontal tensions, obtain speed and period, check dimensional consistency. | A bead traverses a smooth vertical circle: use energy and radial equations to find minimum initial speed for contact at the top, explaining the limiting zero-reaction case. | Equating centripetal force to a new force; omitting weight; confusing tangential/radial directions. |
| **Cambridge 9231 P4** (§4.1, §4.2) | Normalise \`f(x)=kx\`, \`0<x<2\` (else 0); obtain \`k=1/2\`. | For \`f_X(x)=2x\`, \`0<x<1\`, derive the CDF of \`Y=X²\`, its PDF, and check its integral. | A normal-population quality sample \`n=9, x̄=5.8, s=1.2\`: conduct a one-sided \`t\` test of \`μ=5\` at 5%, calculate \`t=2\`, state assumptions and conclusion using df=8. | Treating density as probability at a point; forgetting Jacobian; using normal instead of t with unknown variance. |
| **Pearson 9FM0 Core Pure** (§7.1–7.3 polar) | Convert \`r=4cosθ\` to Cartesian form and identify circle centre/radius. | Find area of the complete cardioid \`r=2(1+cosθ)\` using \`(1/2)∫r²dθ\`; check answer \`6π\`. | A polar sensor footprint is bounded by \`r=2+cosθ\`; determine the area in \`0≤θ≤π\`, compare to a circular footprint of radius 2 and justify which is larger. | Forgetting square on r; wrong angular interval; assuming signed r means signed area. |
| **Pearson 9FM0 FS1** (§2.3, §3.1–3.3) | For success probability 1/2, probability third success occurs on trial 5: \`C(4,2)/2⁵=3/16\`. | For \`p=0.4\`, find the probability the fourth success occurs by trial 8 and compare the mean waiting time with a geometric model. | With \`X~Bin(120,0.02)\`, approximate \`P(X=0)\` by \`Po(2.4)\`, calculate the exact binomial probability, compare errors and explain approximation conditions. | Confusing trial count with failures; negative-binomial support starts at r; Poisson approximation not exact. |
| **Pearson 9FM0 Decision 1** (§2.1, §2.2; 3D/4D only) | On a small original weighted network, identify the next permanently labelled node in Dijkstra's algorithm. | From a fresh six-vertex edge table, construct a minimum spanning tree by Kruskal, state its total weight and compare with a nonminimal proposed tree. | A delivery network closes one edge after a shortest route was found: rerun Dijkstra, justify optimality via permanent labels and quantify the cost of the closure; include a diagram generated from the edge list. | Treating minimum spanning tree as shortest path; finalising a tentative label too soon; using Dijkstra on negative weights. |
