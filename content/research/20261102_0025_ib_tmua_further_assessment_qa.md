## Findings and important board differences
1. **IB cohort split is mandatory.** Current AA/AI mathematics ends with the November 2028 examination; new subject briefs first assessed May 2029. Do not route current approved 2021–2028 items to 2029 merely because the topic title matches. AA 2029 has A3 combinatorics and A4 complex numbers (HL); AI 2029 has D3 probability and C5 graph theory (HL). Broad topic headings in subject briefs are **not** proof of every algorithm-level microobjective; detailed 2029 guide mapping remains an approval gate [S1–S5].
2. **2029 IB removals:** AA SL removes financial applications and bivariate data; AA HL additionally removes counterexample proof and Euler's method. AI SL removes logarithms and trapezoidal area approximations; AI HL removes complex numbers, vector product, Poisson and named hypothesis tests. Current-cohort AI HL Poisson items remain potentially valid for 2028, not for 2029 [S3–S5].
3. **IB assessment differences:** AA 2029 Paper 1 is no-technology, AA Paper 2 technology-required; AI requires technology on every paper. AA and AI SL Papers 1/2: 90 min each; HL Papers 1/2: 120 min each, Paper 3: 60 min. 2029 HL Paper 3 is 50 marks, not the older 55 [S1–S4].
4. **TMUA:** October 2026/January 2027 specification: Paper 1 Section 1 applications, Paper 2 Sections 1+2 argument/logic/proof, 20 MCQ per paper, 75 minutes each, no calculator, no formula booklet, no negative marking. Paper 2 explicitly includes contraposition, necessary/sufficient, negating quantified statements, proof by cases and proof errors; formal symbolic truth tables are not required [S6–S7]. **Do not claim authentic answer-option count** until checked against official current computer specimen.
5. **Cambridge Further 9231:** AS = P1+P3 **or** P1+P4; full A = all P1–P4. P2 is A-only. P1 120 min/75 marks, P3/P4 90 min/50 marks; scientific calculator and MF19 supplied. P1 §1.6 planes, P3 §3.4 springs, P4 §4.1 continuous random variables are distinct assignment lanes. Cambridge warns that AS Further assumes some full A Level 9709 prior knowledge [S8–S9].
6. **Pearson Further 9FM0:** both Core Pure papers are compulsory, optional papers follow valid entry combinations. FS2 is **4B**, not a generic statistics paper; FS2 §1.1–1.2 requires least-squares regression and residual sums of squares. Pearson Issue 4 explicitly corrects an older optional-entry typo: 4D is Decision 2, not Decision 1. Do not confuse with Cambridge Further P4, whose statistics syllabus is different [S10–S11].

## 30 ORIGINAL question blueprints — 10 routes × Daily / Weekly / Exam
**Tier policy:** Daily 2–5 min targeted diagnostic, Weekly 6–10 min linked steps and an explicit check, Exam 8–15 min reasoning-rich multi-mark practice. These are not full mock papers. Prompts and worked answer checkpoints below are authored for MathsGrove; all are drafts until independent review. Preserve written reasoning, not just a one-mark MCQ.

### Lane 1. IB AA SL — first assessment 2029
**Learning objective:** [S1] Topic A3 combinatorics; D2 probability. Broad subject-brief alignment only; confirm exact microobjectives against licensed 2029 guide before release.
**Exam restrictions:** AA Paper 1 no technology; Paper 2 technology required. **Misconceptions:** order versus selection; failing to subtract excluded groups; replacement vs no replacement.
- **Grove Daily (2–5 min, suggested 2 marks):** How many unordered pairs can be selected from 8 distinct books? **Solution checkpoint:** C(8,2)=28; order does not matter.
- **Grove Weekly (6–10 min, suggested 5 marks):** A council selects 4 people from 6 adults and 4 pupils. At least 2 must be pupils. Count possible councils. **Solution checkpoint:** C(4,2)C(6,2)+C(4,3)C(6,1)+C(4,4)=90+24+1=115; disjoint cases.
- **Grove Exam practice (8–15 min, suggested 7–10 marks):** A box contains 5 green and 4 purple counters. Three are chosen uniformly without replacement. Find P(at least 2 purple) and justify the sample space. **Solution/rubric checkpoint:** [C(4,2)C(5,1)+C(4,3)]/C(9,3)=(30+4)/84=17/42. Award sample-space, cases, calculation, interpretation.

### Lane 2. IB AA HL — first assessment 2029
**Learning objective:** [S1] A4 complex numbers (HL), A5 proof and algebraic manipulation, B4 trigonometric functions; detailed De Moivre microobjective to be verified against 2029 guide.
**Exam restrictions:** AA HL P1 no technology; P2/P3 technology required. **Misconceptions:** omitting 2πk; incorrect roots count; signs in triple-angle identity.
- **Grove Daily (2–5 min, suggested 2 marks):** Solve z³=8i, giving all roots in Cartesian form. **Solution checkpoint:** z=2cis(π/6+2πk/3), k=0,1,2: √3+i, −√3+i, −2i.
- **Grove Weekly (6–10 min, suggested 5 marks):** Solve z⁴=−16, giving all four roots. **Solution checkpoint:** z=2cis(π/4+kπ/2), k=0,1,2,3; roots ±√2±i√2 (all sign combinations).
- **Grove Exam practice (8–15 min, suggested 7–10 marks):** Use De Moivre's theorem to derive cos(3θ)=4cos³θ−3cosθ. Hence solve cos(3θ)=0 for 0≤θ<π. **Solution/rubric checkpoint:** Expand (cosθ+i sinθ)³; real part cos³θ−3cosθ sin²θ=4cos³θ−3cosθ. 3θ=π/2,3π/2,5π/2; θ=π/6,π/2,5π/6.

### Lane 3. IB AI SL — first assessment 2029
**Learning objective:** [S2] D3 probability; inquiry: model conditional events and interpret rates. No removed 2029 SL logarithms or trapezoidal rule.
**Exam restrictions:** technology required on AI Paper 1 and Paper 2. **Misconceptions:** reversing conditional probability; ignoring false positives; equating sensitivity with positive predictive value.
- **Grove Daily (2–5 min, suggested 2 marks):** Of 80 pupils, 20 wear glasses; 12 of those 20 play sport. Find P(plays sport | wears glasses). **Solution checkpoint:** 12/20=3/5; denominator is the conditioned group.
- **Grove Weekly (6–10 min, suggested 5 marks):** A condition affects 10% of people. A test detects it 90% of the time and gives a false positive in 20% of unaffected people. Find P(condition | positive). **Solution checkpoint:** P(condition∩positive)=0.09; P(positive)=0.09+0.18=0.27; posterior 1/3.
- **Grove Exam practice (8–15 min, suggested 7–10 marks):** Prevalence is 5%. Test A has sensitivity 90%, false-positive rate 10%; test B has sensitivity 80%, false-positive rate 5%. Compare their positive predictive values and explain which is preferable if avoiding false alarms is the priority. **Solution/rubric checkpoint:** A=0.045/(0.045+0.095)=9/28≈0.3214; B=0.04/(0.04+0.0475)=16/35≈0.4571. B has higher PPV despite lower sensitivity; discuss decision context.

### Lane 4. IB AI HL — first assessment 2029
**Learning objective:** [S2] C5 graph theory (HL). Algorithms (shortest path/MST) are proposed extensions; verify explicit 2029 guide content before release.
**Exam restrictions:** technology required on all AI papers. **Misconceptions:** sum of degrees is 2E; confusing shortest path with fewest edges; greedy selection creating a cycle.
- **Grove Daily (2–5 min, suggested 2 marks):** A finite undirected graph has vertex degrees 2, 3, 3 and 4. How many edges does it have? **Solution checkpoint:** (2+3+3+4)/2=6 by handshaking.
- **Grove Weekly (6–10 min, suggested 5 marks):** An undirected weighted graph has edges AB=2, AC=6, BC=1, BD=5 and CD=2. Find the shortest A-to-D route. **Solution checkpoint:** A–B–C–D has length 2+1+2=5, compared with A–B–D=7 and A–C–D=8.
- **Grove Exam practice (8–15 min, suggested 7–10 marks):** A connected graph has edges AB=4, AC=3, BC=2, BD=5, CD=6, CE=7, DE=1 and BE=8. Find a minimum spanning tree and justify why no cycle is formed. **Solution/rubric checkpoint:** Choose DE=1, BC=2, AC=3, BD=5: all five vertices connected, no cycle, total weight 11. Compare cut/cycle decisions.

### Lane 5. TMUA Paper 1 — Oct 2026/Jan 2027
**Learning objective:** [S6] MM2.1 sequences, MM2.2 arithmetic series, MM2.3 finite and convergent geometric series; application of AS-level pure mathematics.
**Exam restrictions:** 75 minutes, 20 MCQ, no calculator or formula booklet. **Misconceptions:** confusing nth term and partial sum; using wrong ratio; treating alternating partial sums as monotone.
- **Grove Daily (2–5 min, suggested 2 marks):** Evaluate 3+6+12+24+48 without a calculator. **Solution checkpoint:** 93.
- **Grove Weekly (6–10 min, suggested 5 marks):** A geometric sequence begins 2,6,18,... . The sum of its first n terms is 242. Find n. **Solution checkpoint:** S_n=2(3^n−1)/(3−1)=3^n−1=242; 3^n=243; n=5.
- **Grove Exam practice (8–15 min, suggested 7–10 marks):** A geometric series begins 4,−2,1,−1/2,... . Derive S_n and prove 2≤S_n≤4 for every integer n≥1; find the limiting sum. **Solution/rubric checkpoint:** S_n=(8/3)[1−(−1/2)^n]. For even n, min S_2=2; for odd n, max S_1=4; limit 8/3. MCQ adaptation must use a single keyed conclusion.

### Lane 6. TMUA Paper 2 — Oct 2026/Jan 2027
**Learning objective:** [S6] Arg1 contraposition, Arg2 necessary/sufficient, Arg3–4 quantifiers/negation, Prf1 proof by cases, Err1 flawed proofs.
**Exam restrictions:** 75 minutes, 20 MCQ, no calculator; avoid formal truth tables and symbolic logic notation. **Misconceptions:** confusing converse and contrapositive; assuming examples prove universality; dropping one zero-product branch.
- **Grove Daily (2–5 min, suggested 2 marks):** State the contrapositive of: 'If an integer is divisible by 6, then it is divisible by 3.' **Solution checkpoint:** If an integer is not divisible by 3, then it is not divisible by 6.
- **Grove Weekly (6–10 min, suggested 5 marks):** For an integer n, prove that n² is divisible by 4 if and only if n is even. **Solution checkpoint:** If n=2k, n²=4k². If n odd, n=2k+1 and n²=4k(k+1)+1, not divisible by 4.
- **Grove Exam practice (8–15 min, suggested 7–10 marks):** A purported proof claims: 'For all real x,y, x²=y² implies x=y, because (x−y)(x+y)=0 and hence x−y=0.' Identify the precise error, give a counterexample and replace the claim with a true statement. **Solution/rubric checkpoint:** Zero-product rule allows x−y=0 OR x+y=0. Counterexample x=2,y=−2. Correct: x²=y² iff x=y or x=−y. For MCQ require one defensible explanation and plausible wrong-method distractors.

### Lane 7. Cambridge Further 9231 Paper 1 — 2028–2030
**Learning objective:** [S8] §1.6 vectors: Cartesian/vector equations of planes and line-plane intersections; AS and A-level route.
**Exam restrictions:** scientific calculator permitted, MF19 supplied; 120 min / 75 marks. **Misconceptions:** normal vector vs direction vector; incorrect substitution; mixing plane angle with supplementary angle.
- **Grove Daily (2–5 min, suggested 2 marks):** State a normal vector to the plane 2x−y+2z=5. **Solution checkpoint:** (2,−1,2).
- **Grove Weekly (6–10 min, suggested 5 marks):** Find where r=(1,0,2)+t(1,2,−1) intersects x+y+z=6. **Solution checkpoint:** 3+2t=6 gives t=3/2, point (5/2,3,1/2).
- **Grove Exam practice (8–15 min, suggested 7–10 marks):** Find the line of intersection of x+y+z=3 and x−y+z=1. Also find the acute angle between the planes. **Solution/rubric checkpoint:** Subtract gives y=1; x+z=2, so (x,y,z)=(t,1,2−t). Normals (1,1,1) and (1,−1,1), dot 1, each norm √3: acute angle arccos(1/3).

### Lane 8. Cambridge Further 9231 Paper 3 — 2028–2030
**Learning objective:** [S8] §3.4 Hooke's law; extension, modulus and elastic energy; Mechanics paper for AS option or full A-level.
**Exam restrictions:** scientific calculator permitted, MF19; 90 min / 50 marks; state g=10 where needed. **Misconceptions:** confusing modulus with spring constant; missing natural length denominator; elastic energy versus force.
- **Grove Daily (2–5 min, suggested 2 marks):** An elastic spring has natural length 2 m and modulus 60 N. Find tension at extension 0.10 m. **Solution checkpoint:** T=λx/l=60(0.10)/2=3 N.
- **Grove Weekly (6–10 min, suggested 5 marks):** A spring has modulus 80 N, natural length 2 m and extension 0.50 m. Find tension and stored elastic energy. **Solution checkpoint:** T=80(0.5)/2=20 N; E=λx²/(2l)=80(0.25)/4=5 J.
- **Grove Exam practice (8–15 min, suggested 7–10 marks):** Two vertical springs act in parallel, each of natural length 1 m, with moduli 60 N and 40 N. A 2 kg mass hangs at equilibrium; take g=10 m/s². Find the common extension, stretched length and combined elastic energy. **Solution/rubric checkpoint:** (60+40)x/1=20 gives x=0.20 m; length 1.20 m; E=(60+40)x²/(2×1)=2 J. Assumes both springs taut and share extension.

### Lane 9. Cambridge Further 9231 Paper 4 — 2028–2030
**Learning objective:** [S8] §4.1 continuous random variables: PDFs, probabilities, expectations and variance; Statistics paper for AS option or full A-level.
**Exam restrictions:** scientific calculator permitted, MF19; 90 min / 50 marks. **Misconceptions:** density not probability; forgetting normalisation; E(X²)−E(X)² rather than E(X²)−[E(X²)]².
- **Grove Daily (2–5 min, suggested 2 marks):** A random variable has density f(x)=kx for 0≤x≤2 and zero elsewhere. Find k. **Solution checkpoint:** 1=∫₀²kx dx=2k; k=1/2.
- **Grove Weekly (6–10 min, suggested 5 marks):** A random variable has density f(x)=2x/9 for 0≤x≤3. Find E(X) and P(X>2). **Solution checkpoint:** E(X)=∫₀³2x²/9 dx=2; P(X>2)=1−4/9=5/9.
- **Grove Exam practice (8–15 min, suggested 7–10 marks):** A random variable has density f(x)=3x²/8 for 0≤x≤2. Find P(X<1), E(X), Var(X) and comment on whether the density is symmetric. **Solution/rubric checkpoint:** P(X<1)=1/8; E(X)=3/2; E(X²)=12/5; Var=12/5−9/4=3/20. Density increases with x and is not symmetric.

### Lane 10. Pearson Edexcel Further 9FM0 Paper 4B FS2
**Learning objective:** [S10] Further Statistics 2 §1.1 least-squares linear regression and §1.2 residuals and RSS; only for valid optional 4B route.
**Exam restrictions:** calculator permitted; full optional paper 90 min / 75 marks; confirm entry option. **Misconceptions:** regression y on x vs x on y; residual observed−predicted; interpreting RSS without checking model context.
- **Grove Daily (2–5 min, suggested 2 marks):** A model predicts 7.5 units, but the observed value is 9 units. Find the residual. **Solution checkpoint:** Observed−predicted=9−7.5=1.5.
- **Grove Weekly (6–10 min, suggested 5 marks):** For points (1,2),(2,3),(3,5), find the least-squares regression of y on x and predict y when x=4. **Solution checkpoint:** x̄=2, ȳ=10/3, Sxx=2, Sxy=3; slope 3/2, intercept 1/3; ŷ=1/3+(3/2)x; at x=4, ŷ=19/3.
- **Grove Exam practice (8–15 min, suggested 7–10 marks):** For x=0,1,2,3 and y=1,2,2,5, compare model A: ŷ=x+1 with the least-squares model of y on x. Calculate both residual sums of squares and explain a limitation of extrapolating to x=10. **Solution/rubric checkpoint:** A residuals 0,0,−1,1; RSS=2. Least-squares: x̄=3/2, ȳ=5/2, Sxx=5, Sxy=6, ŷ=(6/5)x+7/10; residuals 3/10,1/10,−11/10,7/10; RSS=9/5. Least-squares fits observed points better; x=10 outside range so prediction unreliable.
