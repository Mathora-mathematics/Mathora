## Board-specific objective and misconception gates
- **Cambridge 9709 P4:** 4.1 forces, equilibrium, friction F≤μR; 4.2 one-dimensional kinematics, signed v–t area and calculus; 4.3 direct-impact momentum, including coalescence; 4.4 Newton and connected bodies; 4.5 work, energy, power. Cambridge P4 question papers do **not** use vector notation, and impulse/restitution are **not required**. The paper specifies g=10 m/s² where numerical g is needed. Typical misconceptions: friction always equals μR even when static and not limiting; distance = signed displacement; kinetic energy conserved in coalescence.
- **Cambridge 9709 P5:** 5.1 representation, coded summary statistics; 5.2 linear permutations/combinations (not circular arrangements); 5.3 conditional probability; 5.4 discrete E(X), Var(X), B(n,p), Geo(p) supported on positive integers; 5.5 normal and continuity-corrected binomial approximation. Misconceptions: geometric counts failures rather than trial number; replacement assumption; conditional sample-space denominator.
- **Cambridge 9709 P6:** 6.1 Poisson and approximation; 6.2 linear combinations (variance addition needs independence); 6.3 density integration; 6.4 sampling, estimation, CLT/confidence intervals; 6.5 tests and Type I/II. **Explicit CDF knowledge is not required** for P6; do not import Pearson S2 named F(x) questions. Misconceptions: adding standard deviations instead of variances; treating a density height as a point probability.
- **Pearson UK 9MA0/03 Statistics:** 1.1 sampling, 2.2–2.4 interpreting regression, correlation, dispersion and outliers; 3.2 conditional probability; 4.1 binomial and 4.2 normal; 5.1 correlation test with supplied critical value/p-value, 5.2 binomial tests, 5.3 normal-mean test with known/assumed variance. **Calculating discrete random-variable E/Var excluded**; **CLT not required**; regression line *calculation* excluded. Misconceptions: correlation implies causation; p-value as probability null is true; using incorrect tail.
- **Pearson UK 9MA0/03 Mechanics:** 6.1 units; 7.2 v–t areas; 7.3–7.5 2D vector kinematics, time calculus, projectiles; 8.4 connected particles; 8.6 friction; 9.1 moments. Default g=9.8 m/s² unless specified. Misconceptions: i/j velocity components are speeds; horizontal and vertical velocities vanish simultaneously; using the wrong g.
- **Pearson IAL WME01 M1:** 2.1 planar vectors; 3.1 straight-line constant acceleration; 4.1–4.4 Newton, connected particles, impulse; 5.1–5.3 statics/friction; 6.1 simple parallel-force moments. Restitution belongs to M2, not M1. Misconceptions: moment sign and lever arm; direction of friction; tension versus normal reaction.
- **Pearson IAL WST01 S1:** 2.1–2.4 data and coded measures; 3.1–3.4 probability; 4.1–4.3 regression/PMCC; 5.1–5.4 discrete distributions and E/Var; 6.1 normal. PMCC significance tests are not required in S1. Misconceptions: E(X²)=(E(X))²; confusing conditional denominators; mistaking standard deviation for variance.
- **Pearson IAL WME02 M2:** 1.1–1.4 2D kinematics/calculus; 2.1–2.3 centres of mass, composite lamina; 3.1 work/energy; 4.1–4.3 impact and restitution 0≤e≤1; 5.1–5.2 rigid-body statics. Requires M1 and pure prerequisites; no automatic M1-to-M2 question leakage. Misconceptions: KE conserved at e<1; average of coordinates instead of mass-weighted centroid.
- **Pearson IAL WST02 S2:** 1.1–1.3 binomial/Poisson; 2.2–2.5 PDF and **explicit CDF** F(x), continuous moments/quantiles; 3.1 continuous uniform; 4.1–4.6 tests, including Poisson-mean tests. Requires S1. Misconceptions: f and F interchangeable; forgetting piecewise domain; P(X=x)>0 for continuous X.

**Cross-route validation:** Cambridge 2028–2030 9709 states no significant changes affecting teaching, but that is **not** permission to omit exam-year version tags. Pearson UK 9MA0 is linear, three 100-mark papers, whereas IAL is modular, with unit-specific 75-mark papers. Cambridge A Level allows P1+P3+P4+P5 or P1+P3+P5+P6; **P4+P6 is invalid** and AS P1+P2 cannot be carried forward. Do not present Grove Exam practice as a full timed mock.

## Original question blueprint register — 27 independently constructed seeds
All items: **editorial only**, with target duration Daily 2–5 min, Weekly 6–11 min, Exam reasoning-rich 10–15 min. Suggested marks are *design targets*, not official mark allocations. Exact solutions/checks here are for seed validation; independent human moderation and full marking rubrics are still required.

### Route A — Cambridge 9709 Paper 4 Mechanics (g=10; no vector notation in prompt)
- **C4-D (4.4, 3 min, 3 marks):** A 3 kg trolley moves horizontally under a 12 N driving force and a constant opposing 3 N resistance. Find its acceleration. **Check:** (12−3)/3=3 m/s². **Trap:** dividing 12 by 3 without subtracting resistance.
- **C4-W (4.1/4.4, 9 min, 7 marks):** A 2 kg crate moves **up** a rough slope with sinθ=3/5, cosθ=4/5 and μ=1/4. A constant 25 N force acts up the slope; g=10. Draw a free-body diagram, find normal reaction, friction and acceleration, and check direction. **Check:** R=16 N, friction=4 N down slope, gravity component=12 N, a=(25−4−12)/2=4.5 m/s² up slope. **Trap:** reversing friction or resolving mg using cos instead of sin.
- **C4-E (4.3, 12 min, 10 marks):** A 2 kg cart moves right at 7 m/s and collides directly with a 3 kg cart moving left at 2 m/s. They coalesce. Find signed common velocity, initial and final kinetic energies, loss of KE, and explain which quantity is conserved. **Check:** v=8/5 m/s right, KE_before=55 J, KE_after=6.4 J, loss=48.6 J. **Trap:** conserving KE in a perfectly inelastic impact; importing restitution (not required).

### Route B — Cambridge 9709 Paper 5 Probability & Statistics 1
- **C5-D (5.4, 3 min, 3 marks):** Independent inspections each pass with probability 1/4. Let X be the number of inspections up to and including the first pass. Find P(X=4). **Check:** (3/4)^3(1/4)=27/256. **Trap:** using a binomial model for four trials with exactly one success.
- **C5-W (5.2, 8 min, 7 marks):** Six distinct books are placed in a line. Two specified books must **not** be adjacent. Count valid arrangements using complement and explain why the pair has two internal orders. **Check:** 6!−2·5!=480. **Trap:** treating the pair as a single fixed-order block.
- **C5-E (5.3, 12 min, 10 marks):** A box has 4 red, 3 blue and 2 green counters. Three are drawn without replacement. Given that **at least one is blue**, find the conditional probability that exactly two are red and one is blue. Show a combinations method and a denominator check. **Check:** favourable=6·3=18, conditioned sample space=C(9,3)−C(6,3)=64, answer 9/32. **Trap:** using all C(9,3) outcomes in the denominator.

### Route C — Cambridge 9709 Paper 6 Probability & Statistics 2 (P5 prerequisite)
- **C6-D (6.1, 3 min, 3 marks):** A count X has Poisson mean 3. Find P(X=0), state its variance and explain why these have different units. **Check:** e^(−3); Var(X)=3. **Trap:** setting P(X=0)=0 because the mean is 3.
- **C6-W (6.1, 8 min, 7 marks):** Independent counts X~Po(2) and Y~Po(3) refer to disjoint intervals. Find P(X+Y≤1) and explain the independence assumption. **Check:** X+Y~Po(5), probability=6e^(−5)≈0.0404277. **Trap:** adding probabilities P(X≤1)+P(Y≤1).
- **C6-E (6.2, 12 min, 10 marks):** Independent normal measurements X~N(10,4) and Y~N(12,9). A device triggers when Y−X>0. Derive the distribution of D=Y−X, calculate trigger probability, and explain what fails if X and Y are correlated. **Check:** D~N(2,13); P(D>0)=Φ(2/√13)≈0.710450. **Trap:** subtracting variances or ignoring covariance.

### Route D — Pearson UK 9MA0/03 Statistics
- **UKS-D (1.1, 3 min, 3 marks):** Of 800 learners, 480 study pathway A. A stratified random sample of 50 is required. Find the A allocation and state one random-selection requirement. **Check:** 50·480/800=30. **Trap:** selecting 30 volunteers rather than sampling randomly within the stratum.
- **UKS-W (4.2, 8 min, 7 marks):** A calibrated measurement X~N(80,6²). Find P(74<X<92), show both z-scores and explain why 6, not 36, is the standard deviation. **Check:** Φ(2)−Φ(−1)≈0.818595. **Trap:** treating 6² as SD.
- **UKS-E (5.1, 12 min, 10 marks):** Ten paired observations give sample PMCC r=0.62. Test H0:ρ=0 against H1:ρ>0 at 5%, with the supplied appropriate **one-sided** critical value 0.549. State the decision, interpret it in context and explain why causation does not follow. **Check:** 0.62>0.549, reject H0; evidence of positive population correlation, not causality. **Trap:** testing population mean μ, or treating r as proof of a causal effect. (Critical value is supplied in the item, not computed by student.)

### Route E — Pearson UK 9MA0/03 Mechanics (g=9.8 unless stated)
- **UKM-D (7.4, 3 min, 3 marks):** A particle moves in a straight line with v(t)=6t−2t² m/s. Find acceleration at t=2 s and interpret its negative sign. **Check:** a=6−4t, a(2)=−2 m/s². **Trap:** negative acceleration always means decreasing speed.
- **UKM-W (7.3, 9 min, 8 marks):** A robot has initial velocity (4i+j) m/s and constant acceleration (2i−3j) m/s². Calculate displacement after 3 s, its magnitude and the units. **Check:** displacement=21i−10.5j m; magnitude=10.5√5≈23.479 m. **Trap:** adding magnitudes before applying SUVAT.
- **UKM-E (8.4, 12 min, 10 marks):** Two masses 4 kg and 6 kg are connected by a light string over a smooth pulley, released from rest. Find acceleration, string tension and distance travelled in 2 s. Explain why tension differs from either weight. **Check:** a=1.96 m/s² (6 kg down), T=47.04 N, s=3.92 m. **Trap:** T=(4+6)g or treating both masses as accelerating downward.

### Route F — Pearson IAL WME01 Mechanics M1 (g=9.8 specified)
- **M1-D (2.1, 3 min, 3 marks):** A horizontal resultant force is (3i+4j) N. Find magnitude and direction above the positive i-axis. **Check:** 5 N; arctan(4/3)≈53.13°. **Trap:** treating components as magnitudes to add directly.
- **M1-W (6.1, 9 min, 8 marks):** A uniform horizontal 4 m rod of mass 5 kg is hinged at its left end. A vertical cable supports its right end; an additional 2 kg mass hangs 3 m from the hinge. In equilibrium, find cable tension and vertical hinge reaction. **Check:** 4T=5g·2+2g·3, T=39.2 N, hinge vertical reaction=29.4 N upward. **Trap:** placing rod's weight at an end rather than its midpoint.
- **M1-E (5.3/4.2, 12 min, 10 marks):** A 2 kg body moves **up** a plane with sinθ=3/5, cosθ=4/5 and coefficient of friction 0.20. A constant applied force P acts up the plane and produces acceleration 1.5 m/s² up the plane. Find P and explain why the friction direction is known. **Check:** P=2(1.5)+2(9.8)(3/5)+0.2·2(9.8)(4/5)=17.896 N. **Trap:** using friction up the plane or g=10 by habit.

### Route G — Pearson IAL WST01 Statistics S1
- **S1-D (2.2, 3 min, 3 marks):** Values are coded by y=(x−5)/2. If the original mean is 13, find the coded mean and explain the affine transformation. **Check:** (13−5)/2=4. **Trap:** dividing before subtracting.
- **S1-W (5.1–5.3, 9 min, 8 marks):** X takes 0,1,3 with probabilities a,2a,1−3a. Given E(X)=1.6, find a, verify the probabilities and calculate Var(X). **Check:** 3−7a=1.6 so a=0.2; E(X²)=4; Var=4−1.6²=1.44. **Trap:** squaring the expectation in place of E(X²).
- **S1-E (6.1, 11 min, 9 marks):** Independent fill weights are N(50,5²) grams. Determine the cutoff for the heaviest 10% and the probability that at least one of two independently chosen fills exceeds it. Explain why independence matters. **Check:** cutoff≈56.408 g; 1−0.9²=0.19. **Trap:** using the lower-tail 10th percentile or adding 0.1+0.1 without overlap adjustment.

### Route H — Pearson IAL WME02 Mechanics M2 (requires M1 and Pure prerequisites)
- **M2-D (4.2, 3 min, 3 marks):** A particle strikes a fixed smooth wall normally at 6 m/s; coefficient of restitution e=0.4. Find its rebound speed and direction. **Check:** 2.4 m/s away from wall. **Trap:** retaining original direction after impact.
- **M2-W (2.1, 9 min, 8 marks):** Point masses 2 kg at (0,0), 3 kg at (4,0) and 5 kg at (0,6) are joined by light rods. Find centre of mass and explain why an unweighted average is wrong. **Check:** (1.2,3) m. **Trap:** averaging three coordinates without weighting by mass.
- **M2-E (4.1–4.2, 13 min, 11 marks):** A 2 kg particle moving right at 5 m/s strikes a stationary 3 kg particle in a direct impact with e=0.5. Find both signed post-impact velocities and kinetic energy loss, and verify 0≤e≤1. **Check:** v_1=0.5 m/s right, v_2=3 m/s right, loss=11.25 J. **Trap:** conserving kinetic energy when e<1.

### Route I — Pearson IAL WST02 Statistics S2 (requires S1)
- **S2-D (2.2, 3 min, 3 marks):** f(x)=kx for 0<x<2, zero elsewhere. Determine k from normalisation and explain why f(x) itself is not a point probability. **Check:** k=1/2. **Trap:** setting f(1)=P(X=1).
- **S2-W (2.2–2.3, 9 min, 8 marks):** A continuous distribution has F(x)=0 for x≤0, F(x)=x²/9 for 0<x<3 and F(x)=1 for x≥3. Find f(x), P(1<X<2), and check endpoint continuity. **Check:** f(x)=2x/9 on (0,3), probability=(4−1)/9=1/3. **Trap:** differentiating the wrong branch or failing to use F(2)−F(1).
- **S2-E (2.2/2.4/2.5, 13 min, 11 marks):** A density is f(x)=cx(4−x) for 0<x<4 and zero elsewhere. Determine c, mean, median, and P(X>3). Explain why symmetry is useful and verify nonnegativity. **Check:** c=3/32, E(X)=2, median=2 by symmetry, P(X>3)=5/32. **Trap:** treating the polynomial as valid outside [0,4] or mistaking the mode for a point mass.
